What we mean by a "full bathroom renovation" 
A full bathroom renovation is a strip-out-to-studs rebuild.  
 
Everything comes out — tiles, fixtures, the waterproofing membrane, sometimes the joinery 
and walls themselves — and the room is rebuilt from the substrate up. This is different from 
a "bathroom refresh" (replacing fixtures and tapware while keeping the existing membrane 
and tiling) and different again from a "reconfigure" (moving walls and relocating plumbing).

Why bathroom renovations go over budget and how we prevent it 
Five things cause most Sydney bathroom renovations to drift over budget. Every one of 
them is avoidable with good preparation. 
1.​ Discovering rot, mould or non-compliant plumbing during strip-out. We allow a 
contingency in every quote and inspect substrates as soon as the room is open. 
2.​ Asbestos in pre-1990 homes. Testing before quoting protects the budget; remediation 
is priced separately and transparently. 
3.​ Changing fixture choices mid-build. We lock in PC items before fit-off so substitutions 
are by exception, not by default. 
4.​ Moving plumbing or fixture locations after rough-in. The design is signed off before 
rough-in begins. 
5.​ PC items chosen outside the quoted allowance. Allowances are clearly stated in 
writing, so the budget for each tile, tap and basin is on the page.

Frequently asked questions 
Do I need council approval for a bathroom renovation in Sydney? 
Most like-for-like bathroom renovations within an existing footprint do not require 
development consent under the State Environmental Planning Policy (Exempt and 
Complying Development Codes) 2008. Renovations that move walls, alter the external 
envelope, or sit inside a heritage conservation area may require approval. Heritage-listed 
properties almost always do. We assess this at the on-site quote stage so you know before 
signing. 
 
Can I live in the house during the renovation? 
Yes, in most cases. If yours is the only bathroom in the home, we discuss a temporary 
arrangement up front rather than mid-build. The strip-out week is the most disruptive — dust, 
noise, no access to the room — so households with one bathroom usually plan around that 
week specifically. 
 
Do you supply the products, or do I? 
Either works. Our designer can curate fixtures, tiles and joinery for you from our trusted 
supplier network, or you can source products yourself. If you supply your own products, we 
review them against the design and the build before they arrive on site so there are no 
incompatibility surprises.

Every Elite Touch bathroom is delivered to the current Australian Standards relevant to 
wet-area work — AS 3740 (waterproofing), AS/NZS 3500 (plumbing), AS/NZS 3000 
(electrical wiring rules), AS 1288 and AS/NZS 2208 (safety glass), AS 4586 (slip resistance), 
and the National Construction Code Volume Two. WELS-registered fixtures are used 
throughout for water efficiency. 
 
Is waterproofing really that important? 
It is the single most important part of the build. A failed waterproofing membrane is the 
leading cause of bathroom-related damage to Sydney homes — mould inside cavities, rotted 
framing, damaged ceilings on the floor below. AS 3740 sets the technical requirements, and 
a Certificate of Compliance from the waterproofer is issued for every Elite Touch bathroom. 
 
What warranty do I get? 
In NSW, residential building work is covered by the statutory warranties under section 18B of 
the Home Building Act 1989 — six years for major defects and two years for non-major 
defects from the date of completion. Specific products (tapware, electricals, joinery) carry 
their own manufacturer warranties, which we hand to you at completion.
