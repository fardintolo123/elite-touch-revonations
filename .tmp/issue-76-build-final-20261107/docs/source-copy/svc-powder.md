Powder Room Renovations Sydney 
The smallest room in the house. Often the one your guests notice most. 
 
A powder room is the smallest bathroom in the home. There is no shower, usually no bath 
but just a toilet and a basin, sometimes inside a footprint smaller than a wardrobe. That 
constraint is a creative challenge because powder rooms are where homeowners can 
sometimes be bold: statement wallpaper, dramatic tile, a sculptural basin, a piece of art that 
would not work anywhere else in the house.  
 
They can be like a jewel box and they are the room your guests will spend the most 
uninterrupted time looking at. Or you have the freedom to build something simple and 
functional, we can build it any way you like. 
What is a powder room? 
A powder room is a small bathroom containing a toilet and a hand basin and usually nothing 
else. It is sometimes called a "half bath" or "guest WC". The footprint is typically 1.5–3 
square metres, and it is almost always located on the ground floor near common living areas 
where guests can use it without entering the private parts of the home. 
What a powder room is not: 
•​
An ensuite (which sits inside a private bedroom) 
•​
A main bathroom (which contains a shower and/or bath) 
•​
A laundry (which contains laundry appliances and a tub) 
Why powder rooms reward design ambition 
The maths of a small room cuts in your favour: 
•​
Small surface area means a single feature can carry the whole space. A bold 
wallpaper that would overwhelm a main bathroom is right at home in 2 square 
metres. 
•​
Premium materials become affordable. A $300/sqm tile across a small wall costs less 
than the same tile in a full bathroom so the splurge is realistic rather than 
aspirational. 
•​
No shower means no wet-zoning to design around. The constraints that shape a full 
bathroom (water exposure, glass, drying surfaces) do not apply. The design has 
more freedom. 
•​
Guests notice. The powder room is the one bathroom many visitors will ever see in 
your home, so the investment shows publicly. 
What a powder room renovation includes 
The scope is smaller than a full bathroom, but every element still has to be done properly: 
•​
Strip-out of existing fixtures, joinery, and finishes 
•​
Wet-area waterproofing to AS 3740-2021 (yes, it is still required) 
•​
Plumbing for the WC and basin by a NSW-licensed plumber

Compliance is smaller in scope but not optional 
A powder room is a wet area under AS 3740 because it contains a floor waste and a 
water-supply fixture. The waterproofing scope is narrower than a full bathroom — no shower 
walls or hob — but the floor still requires a compliant, certified membrane. The plumbing and 
electrical work is licensed work, exactly as it would be in any other wet area.

Three Sydney powder room scenarios we see often 
1. The under-stair powder room in an inner-city terrace 
Carved into the space beneath the staircase in a Victorian or federation terrace. Tight 
footprint, sloped ceiling, heritage character to respect, and ventilation routing that has to 
navigate the staircase above. Often the most rewarding to design — the constraints force 
creative answers. 
2. The apartment guest powder room 
Common in larger Sydney apartments. The challenges are usually strata-related — 
body-corporate approval for any plumbing change, common-wall constraints, and limited 
venting options. We handle the strata correspondence at the quote stage. 
3. The family-home main-floor powder room 
Standard suburban scenario — a small room off the entrance hall or near the living areas. 
The brief is usually a step-change in design quality from what the builder originally installed. 
Most family-home powder rooms get heavier use than the owners expect, so finishes need 
to handle real wear. 
Design moves that work in small rooms 
•​
One bold wall (tile, wallpaper, or panelling) with the rest of the room held in a quieter 
neutral 
•​
Wall-hung toilet to lift the visual weight off the floor and make the room feel larger 
•​
A vessel basin or wall-mounted basin instead of a vanity, where storage is not the 
priority 
•​
Mirror as a deliberate piece that is large, framed, or shaped rather than a 
builder-grade rectangle 
•​
Considered lighting: a single statement pendant, or a sconce pair flanking the mirror, 
rather than a downlight 
•​
Heritage detailing (skirting, architrave, panelling) where the home suits it

Frequently asked questions 
Do I need council approval to add a powder room? 
Often yes, if the powder room is new (rather than a renovation of an existing one). Adding a 
new wet area typically requires either complying-development approval under SEPP 
(Exempt and Complying Development Codes) or, in some cases, a full development 
application — particularly in heritage conservation areas. We assess the approval pathway 
at the on-site quote. 
 
Does a powder room need ventilation? 
Yes. The National Construction Code Volume Two requires ventilation in wet areas, and 
most Sydney powder rooms are internal so cannot rely on a window. Mechanical extraction 
sized to the room volume per AS 1668.2 is the standard approach. 
 
Can a powder room add value to my home? 
A well-designed, well-finished powder room is one of the strongest per-dollar value adds in a 
Sydney home, particularly in a property that lacks a downstairs WC. It serves guests, 
removes the social awkwardness of guests using a bedroom-adjacent main bathroom, and 
adds a design moment to the public part of the house.

What is the smallest a powder room can be? 
Practically, around 0.8 m wide by 1.5 m deep — enough for a basin, a WC, and a turning 
circle for the door. Tighter than that and the room starts to feel claustrophobic and may 
struggle to comply with the minimum clearances around fixtures. 
 
Do powder rooms really need waterproofing? 
Yes — under AS 3740-2021 any room with a floor waste and a water-supply fixture is a wet 
area, and the floor requires a compliant, certified waterproof membrane. The scope is 
smaller than a full bathroom (no shower walls, no hob), but the floor is non-negotiable. 
 
Can a powder room be done in less than a week? 
A refresh — same fixtures replaced like-for-like, same finishes, new paint — can be done in 
three to five working days. A full renovation requiring strip-out and re-waterproofing needs 
the membrane to cure properly before tiling, which puts the realistic minimum at around 
seven working days.
