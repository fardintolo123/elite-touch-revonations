## Anti-Patterns Verdict

The homepage does not look like a generic AI landing page. It uses real project photography, verified business facts, restrained language, an identifiable visual system, and specific process evidence. The automated detector returned zero findings for `app/page.tsx`. No reliable user-visible detector overlay was available because the connected IAB browser was unavailable; visible Playwright desktop and mobile contexts provided the browser evidence instead.

## Overall Impression

The current page already follows most of the 80/20 method. The best opportunity is not a thin funnel redesign; it is to make the existing proof and enquiry path appear in the right order. Desktop is strong. Mobile is materially weakened by a sticky navigation block that consumes roughly 45% of every viewport.

## What's Working

- The desktop hero clearly states service, location, written-scope promise, and next step beside a real Castle Hill project image.
- Proof is unusually credible: verified 5.0/19 Google rating, named verbatim testimonials, licence, AS 3740 certificate, and warranty.
- The page answers the real risks of a high-value renovation: written scope, disruption, timing, hidden conditions, managed trades, and care in the home.

## Priority Issues

### P1 — The sticky mobile navigation dominates the viewport

- **Why it matters:** At 390x844 the header is about 377px tall. The first screen shows navigation and only part of the headline; the booking CTA, Google proof, project image, and named review are below the fold. The same header obscures nearly half the screen while reading the form.
- **Fix:** Collapse the mobile link set behind a native, server-rendered `details` menu while keeping the persistent call button. Preserve keyboard access and all links in the initial HTML.
- **Suggested command:** `$impeccable adapt homepage mobile header`

### P2 — The hero copy is too long for the first screen

- **Why it matters:** The H1 runs four desktop lines and about five mobile lines; the lead also covers pain, quoting, exclusions, waterproofing, tiling, and fit-off. The outcome and next step compete with supporting detail.
- **Fix:** Keep the settled “near you in Sydney” intent in the H1 or equally prominent opening copy, but separate it from the written-scope promise. Use a one-sentence subheadline and move process detail lower.
- **Suggested command:** `$impeccable clarify homepage hero`

### P2 — Real project proof and top objections arrive too late

- **Why it matters:** Recent work begins around 6,510px desktop / 8,827px mobile. Cost and timing context do not appear until the long FAQ. These are stronger decision inputs than another generic service explanation.
- **Fix:** Move the existing recent-work strip directly after the hero. Surface a compact three-concern block for written scope, waterproofing/certificate, and schedule/hidden-condition handling, while retaining the owner-mandated full FAQ lower down.
- **Suggested command:** `$impeccable layout homepage sequence`

### P2 — The primary homepage CTA takes an unnecessary detour

- **Why it matters:** “Book a free on-site measure” opens `/contact-us/`, although the same form exists on the homepage. On mobile the form starts about 10,265px down and its first field about 10,967px down.
- **Fix:** Make the homepage CTA point to `#enquire` and add `scroll-margin-top` for the compact sticky header. Keep `/contact-us/` in navigation and internal links.
- **Suggested command:** `$impeccable polish homepage CTA flow`

### P3 — The seven-control form feels heavier than its actual commitment

- **Why it matters:** Only name, phone, and email are required, but suburb, service, message, and photos are not consistently labelled optional. The visual count therefore reads as seven required decisions.
- **Fix:** Keep the owner-required email and optional photo upload, explicitly label every optional field, explain why email is collected, and test whether progressive disclosure for message/photos improves completion before adding a multi-step form.
- **Suggested command:** `$impeccable clarify enquiry form`

## Persona Red Flags

- **Jordan, first-time renovator:** The mobile first screen delays the main booking action and real proof, so the page asks for patience before it earns trust. “Those three things” in the later proof introduction has no clear antecedent.
- **Casey, distracted mobile user:** A 377px sticky header leaves roughly half a viewport for content, forces excessive scrolling, and covers much of the form. The persistent call CTA is useful, but the measure CTA is not initially reachable.
- **Riley, deliberate checker:** The page is honest about hidden conditions and scope, but “10-year workmanship warranty on everything we build” is broader than the owner-confirmed wording and should be reduced to the exact approved claim.

## Minor Observations

- Replace the area-section sentence about why pages are published with customer-facing coverage language.
- Keep the complete legal footer; it already includes privacy, terms, ABN, licence, AS 3740, warranty, contact details, office context, and hours.
- Keep the existing real image and both hero CTAs. Do not introduce stock imagery, urgency devices, a chat widget, or new dependencies.
- The local dev console showed analytics requests blocked by the current CSP. Verify production analytics separately; it is not a reason to change the landing-page layout.

## Questions to Consider

- Should the first mobile screen optimize for an immediate call, or for a free-measure request after one short proof block?
- Can the written-scope promise carry the hero while pricing, timing, and waterproofing become the three immediate reassurance points beneath it?
- Would explicit “optional” labels remove enough form friction, or does real submission data justify a two-step form later?
