Bathroom and Laundry Renovations Sydney 
Two wet areas, one project, one disruption window. 
 
A bathroom and a laundry are the two most plumbing-intensive rooms in any Sydney home. 
Renovating them together whether they share a wall, sit on different floors, or get folded into 
a single combined space, turns two separate, disruptive renovations into a single project with 
one set of trades on site, one waterproofing process and one final clean.  
 
The saving in disruption is real and the saving in cost is meaningful while creating 
consistency and harmony in different rooms of your home. 
What we mean by "bathroom and laundry renovations" 
The term covers four distinct scenarios. Each one has its own design and build logic: 
•​
Two separate rooms, renovated in parallel. Same trades on site, sequenced across 
both rooms. This is the most common configuration in Sydney homes built between 
1950 and 1990. 
•​
A combined bathroom-laundry — a single back-of-house wet area containing both 
functions. Common in townhouses, apartments, and smaller cottages where floor 
space is tight. 
•​
A bathroom with a concealed Euro laundry — a washer and dryer hidden behind 
cabinetry inside the bathroom. Common in inner-Sydney apartments. 
•​
A bathroom renovation paired with a separate laundry renovation in a different part of 
the house, run as a single program. 
 
Which scenario fits you depends on layout, household size, and how you actually do laundry.  
 
We help you decide at the consultation stage, before scope is locked. 
 
Why combining them works 
Running the bathroom and laundry as one program rather than two separate jobs months 
apart delivers real savings: 
•​
One mobilisation, one demolition window, one final clean. Trades arrive once, set up 
once, leave once. 
•​
A single waterproofing inspection trail rather than two. The waterproofer is on site for 
one continuous treatment, even where the rooms are not adjacent. 
•​
Plumbing efficiencies where the rooms share a wall with fewer wall penetrations, less 
re-routing of in-slab pipework etc 
•​
A single contract, one project, one final inspection. Less administrative overhead for 
you.

Three common Sydney layouts 
1. Adjacent rooms sharing a plumbing wall 
Common in homes built from the 1950s to 1980s. Bathroom and laundry sit either side of a 
shared wall, with both sets of plumbing running through the same vertical chase. This is the 
most cost-effective configuration to renovate together — a single rough-in covers both 
rooms. 
2. Stacked floors, vertical alignment 
In two-storey homes, the upstairs bathroom often sits directly above the downstairs laundry. 
Renovating them together means one stack of pipework can be addressed in a single 
program, with access through the laundry ceiling to the bathroom floor. 
3. Concealed Euro laundry inside the bathroom 
Increasingly common in Sydney apartments and townhouses where dedicated laundry 
space does not exist. A washer-dryer combo or stacked pair hides behind cabinetry, with the 
bathroom otherwise reading as a regular bathroom. Strata approval may be required where 
pipework is being added or moved; we check this at the quote stage. 
What a laundry-specific renovation involves 
A laundry shares much of its compliance framework with a bathroom, it is a wet area under 
AS 3740, but has its own design and build considerations. 
•​
Hot and cold supply to the washing-machine taps, with the cold supply isolatable 
behind the machine 
•​
Floor waste connected to drainage (AS/NZS 3500) 
•​
Power outlets at washer height (typically 10A or 15A depending on appliance) 
installed to AS/NZS 3000 
•​
Dryer venting — required for vented dryers; not required for heat-pump or condenser 
dryers 
•​
Laundry tub or sink, with hot and cold and drain 
•​
Bench space for folding, with storage for laundry products 
•​
Drying line, drying rail, or fold-down rack 
•​
Ventilation to manage steam and humidity 
Hidden vs visible laundry — which is right for you? 
There is a real design choice here, and it matters for resale, daily use, and how the rest of 
the home reads. 
Hidden laundry 
Behind a cabinet door or inside a bathroom.  
Pros: maximises living space, looks tidy, ideal for households that do small frequent washes. 
Cons: less bench space, limited drying area, harder to handle big-load weeks (sheets, 
towels). 
Visible laundry 
A dedicated room with its own design language.  
Pros: handles real-world laundry volume, doubles as a mud room or pantry overflow, adds 
resale value in family-home markets.

Frequently asked questions 
Should I combine my bathroom and laundry into one room? 
It depends on how much you actually use a laundry. Households that do small, frequent 
washes and value living space often prefer a concealed laundry inside the bathroom or 
behind cabinetry. Households that do big weekly washes, especially families with kids, 
benefit from a dedicated, visible laundry room. We talk through your routine at the 
consultation rather than pushing a default. 
 
Does a Euro laundry add or subtract from my home's value? 
In the Sydney apartment and inner-city townhouse market, a well-designed concealed 
laundry is neutral to positive as buyers expect it while in the suburban family-home market, 
removing a dedicated laundry to add a Euro version often subtracts value, because family 
buyers want the space. Resale value is one factor we discuss at your design consultation. 
 
Can I keep using the bathroom while the laundry is renovated separately? 
Yes, if the rooms are sequenced rather than run in parallel. That is one of the advantages of 
doing them together — you can keep one functioning while the other is offline, then swap. 
We design the program around that constraint when it matters to you. 
 
Does my washing machine need a separate water supply? 
No. The washer connects to the same cold (and hot, if your machine uses it) supply that 
serves the rest of the wet area, through dedicated washer taps with isolation valves. A 
NSW-licensed plumber installs and certifies this to AS/NZS 3500. 
 
What is the typical timeline for a combined bathroom-and-laundry project? 
Four to six weeks on site for most Sydney homes, depending on whether the rooms are 
adjacent (cheaper, faster) or in different parts of the house (slower because the trades have 
two work fronts to manage).
